02 — The ground station

Easy to use, focused on the mission, and built to reduce mistakes.

Controls sit where you expect them, the video carries nothing you did not ask for, and the planner checks the route against the ground before the aircraft ever leaves it.

Download for Windows

Fly View — split map and live feed.

Fly View

Fly the aircraft and work the camera at the same time

  • Both halves are live at once — re-task the aircraft on the map while the payload keeps tracking, with no mode to switch between them.
  • Aircraft controls left, payload controls right, each fixed in place, so nothing has to be reached across.
  • Live position against the planned route, with detection running on the feed while it flies.

Several aircraft — three on missions from one station, each with its own camera.

Several aircraft

One station, a whole flight of aircraft

  • Every aircraft on one screen — the Aircraft panel shows each one's flight mode, state, height, speed, battery, GPS and distance from home, and turns its card red the moment its link is lost.
  • Command a group at once — select some or all, then Arm, Start mission, Pause, Return or Land them together, each behind a slide-to-confirm.
  • Take any one in hand — one click makes that aircraft the one the toolbar, instruments and actions follow; the others carry on with their missions.
  • A camera per aircraft — the aircraft in control fills the main picture with the AI running on it, and up to three others play live in tiles below. Click a tile to switch.

Plan View — mission and editable item table.

Plan View

A plan that tells you when it is wrong

  • Terrain collision checked for you — the station pulls real elevation for the route and compares every leg against the ground; the segment that collides is drawn in red, so it's obvious which one.
  • Errors shown against the item that caused them, so a bad altitude is obvious in the table instead of buried in a log.
  • Built by following the prompts — takeoff, waypoints, return, each step leading to the next, so nothing has to be remembered in order.
  • Per-item altitude frame — relative to launch, above sea level, or held above the terrain.
  • Every item editable as a table row, with distance, count and time totalled as the plan changes.

3D View — the terrain check in flight, the live aircraft, and a plan previewed before take-off.

3D View

Understand the mission before you fly it

  • Every leg coloured by its clearance — green clears the ground by 30 m or more, amber comes within 30 m, red passes through it, and the spot is marked route hits ground here.
  • The aircraft's real height over the ground — a line straight down to the terrain with the clearance beside it, and heading, speed, climb, height and distance home in the corner.
  • Alerts where they happened — each AI alert is pinned on the terrain at the place the aircraft was when it was raised.
  • Check a plan before anything flies — in Plan View the mission is shown over the terrain with no aircraft connected.
  • Follow, Top, Orbit or Free camera, with buttons to move, turn, tilt and zoom, and a quality setting for slower computers.
  • Works with no internet — imagery and terrain downloaded beforehand are used from the computer.

Video window — the feed, and nothing else.

Video window

Nothing sitting between the operator and the picture

  • The feed fills the screen — no floating panels, no docked widgets, nothing laid over the part of the frame being watched.
  • Telemetry stays on the bar, so going full-frame costs no aircraft state.
  • Detection boxes are the only overlay, and they switch off when the operator wants the raw picture.
  • Thermal alongside EO — picture-in-picture, full frame, or blended with adjustable opacity.

Two flood rescues — people cut off on a roof, a person in the water — read and raised automatically.

Situational awareness

Not a detector. A model that reads the situation

  • Reads the situation, not just the object — people stranded on a roof in floodwater become “people cut off by water”, a lone swimmer becomes “person in water”, named by the scene model rather than left as a plain box.
  • Severity is assigned, not left to the operator — graded and raised as an alert with a confidence score, ready to be triaged like an incoming report.
  • Position travels with the alert — the zone and coordinates are logged automatically, so a tasking arrives with a location already attached.
  • The same engine, any situation — no separate model per scenario; one detector and rule engine reads whatever the mission puts in front of it, on the live feed as it happens.

Remote Video Terminal — the same feed in a browser, with nothing installed.

Remote Video Terminal

Everyone who needs the picture can have it

  • Nothing to install — the station serves a web page, so any phone, tablet or laptop with a browser can watch. On an incident ground that counts for more than a native client would.
  • Viewers join by QR code — the station can broadcast its own Wi-Fi, so people get on without a network to find or credentials to hand around.
  • The whole picture travels — live video, the AI boxes, the alerts and the telemetry, and the map with the aircraft on it. Not just a video stream.
  • The aircraft is not involved — the copy is taken inside the station, so the radio link, the aircraft and the operator's own display are untouched whether anyone is watching or not.
  • Several drones on one screen — a control room can watch every ground station at once with the free multi-drone viewer.
