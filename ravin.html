01 — The aircraft

Launch like a multirotor. Fly like a wing.

Small UAS usually forces a choice: multirotors launch anywhere and run out of endurance; fixed wings do the opposite and need a runway to prove it. Ravin is designed to sit on its tail, lift off vertically, then pitch over and fly as a wing — the trade-off, gone. It is in development and has not flown yet.

Unibody composite shell

Wing, body and tail formed as one structure — fewer joints, less to fail and less to inspect between sorties.

Twin motor pods per wing

The same units lift the aircraft vertically and drive it forward once it is wing-borne.

Modular sensor bay

A defined volume in the centre body with its own access panel — swap the payload without touching flight structure.

Wingtip lights and skids

Lit for orientation in low light, and doubling as the aircraft's landing contact point.

Launch pose

Standing on its tail

Both propellers point straight up for a vertical take-off. The fin tips and the wing are what it is designed to stand on.

Payload

Camera gimbal in the belly

An EO/IR gimbal ball sits in a 58 mm aperture under the nose, looking forward and down, clear of both propellers.

In development

Ravin, before first flight.

Concept of operations

Backpack to overwatch, without a prepared site.

Launch

Any clear ground

No runway, catapult or launch rail. The aircraft is designed to stand on its tail and climb vertically from the spot it was set down.

Transit

Wing-borne cruise

It is designed to transition to horizontal flight for the mission, trading rotor lift for wing efficiency and the endurance that comes with it.

Recovery

Back onto its tail

It is designed to return to the vertical and settle onto its wingtip skids. No net, no belly landing, no recovery crew.
